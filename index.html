Injected and Leaked:
Actively Inducing Side-channel Leakage Using Electromagnetic Injection and Hardware Non-linearity

Paper #651, submitted to USENIX Security 2026

Overview

This paper discovers InjectEave attack that demonstrates how active EM injection and hardware non-linearity can induce controllable, previously infeasible side-channel leakage, enabling both high-fidelity eavesdropping and closed-loop conversation manipulation. To ground this new threat model in practical settings, we evaluate 11 commercial COTS devices across five categories, including wired and wireless headphones, landline phones, smart fans, and smart lamps, and further include an investigation on analog microphone inputs. This website presents demos and experimental data ranging from laboratory demonstrations to real-world through-wall and long-distance (30 m) eavesdropping case studies, and then to microphone-focused feasibility analysis.

Part I: Demonstrations in a Laboratory Setting

Demo 1: Eavesdropping on Smart Fans' Control Signal

As the fan speed increases, the frequency of the received control signal gradually increases, indicating that the fan's current operating state can be inferred from the received control signal. This enables “Context Inference” attacks: for example, detecting “Sleep Mode” at night can confirm a user’s rest schedule and infer occupancy without the need for visual surveillance.

Demo 2: Eavesdropping on Smart Lamps' Brightness

As the lamp becomes brighter, the intensity of the received power signal gradually increases. By mapping these power levels to vendor-specific presets (e.g., 20% brightness for “Reading”), an adversary can perform “Behavioral Profiling,” transforming a light source into a beacon that exposes a user’s specific activities and routines without requiring any network-level access.

Case Study 3: Closed-loop Conversation Eavesdropping & Manipulation on Landline Devices

Attack Process

Case study 3 follows an "Eavesdrop-Synthesize-Inject" closed-loop pipeline.

Step 1: Eavesdrop. The attacker monitors side channel leakage to obtain call context in real time.

Step 2: Synthesize. When trigger keywords (e.g., "quote" or "confirmation") are detected, IndexTTS-2 synthesizes a forged, malicious response with the same speaker identity.

Step 3: Inject. The synthesized audio is injected into the landline output via EM coupling to maliciously manipulate the victim's decision, for example, altering a response from "agree" to "disagree".

Conversation Texts

  1. Reply: "Understood. Since they are a returning client, quote them 5 million." -> Synthesized: "Actually change it to 8 million."
  2. Reply: "Let me think about it. Yes, I agree." -> Synthesized: "Actually, I disagree."
  3. Reply: "Yes, turn right and you will see the hotel." -> Synthesized: "Sorry, please turn left."

Part V: Investigation on Robustness, Selectivity, and Stealthiness

Demo 6: Robust Eavesdropping Under Multi-device and Ambient RF Interference

The attacker placed the surveillance device in the room adjacent to the victim, eavesdropping on the target at a 3m distance through a 20cm wall. The victim is in a meeting room with multiple operating electronic devices, including cameras, a ceiling microphone array, a central air-conditioning system, and display screens. Several nearby devices operated simultaneously, including two additional audio-playing headphones, two microphones, a lamp, and a fan. The recovered eavesdropped audio remained stable under such interference.

Demo 7: Investigation of Frequency Selectivity and Spatial Selectivity

The experimental setup was initially configured to eavesdrop on PHILIPS TAH2020. By tuning the carrier frequency, the attack was switched to the adjacent UGreen MAX2, showing that different headphone models can be distinguished by their distinct frequency bands. Finally, by realigning the antennas, the attack was switched to the UGreen MAX2 on the right, showing that spatial separation maximizes isolation between the antennas and enables selectivity between two headphones of the same model.

Stealthiness of Single-tone Injection

The stealthiness of the injection may be compromised by the detectable artifacts in the output audio especially when the injection strength is very strong. To assess whether the injected tone is detectable even in a worst case condition, we specifically place the transmitting antenna adjacent to the target headphone (UGreen MAX2) and radiate the single-tone injection carrier at 18 dBm power, while ensuring this setting can induce recoverable leakage.

Part VI: Investigation on High Injection Power

Demo 8: Long-distance Attack

By amplifying the injection power from 18 dBm to 40 dBm via an external power amplifier (G41P40S), the system maintains successful recognition of spoken digit sequences at distances up to 30 m.
